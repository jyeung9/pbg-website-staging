Play-Based Therapy

Play-based therapy builds learning into the things your child already loves, including play, songs, snacks, and everyday routines.

What play-based ABA looks like for young children

Instead of spending most of the session at a table, we follow your child's interests and build learning into everyday interactions. Therapy is woven into play, relationships, and everyday activities your child already enjoys.

Quick answers for parents

Who is play-based therapy for?

Play-based therapy is especially well suited for toddlers and preschool-aged children, although many of the same principles remain useful as children get older.

How is it different from traditional ABA?

It follows your child's interests and builds learning into natural play and everyday interactions, rather than relying primarily on structured, adult-directed activities. Both approaches are rooted in the science of applied behavior analysis. Play-based therapy places greater emphasis on relationships, shared engagement, and learning within everyday activities.

Do parents participate?

Absolutely. We'll work with you to build learning into the routines and activities your family already experiences every day, so your child has opportunities to practice new skills beyond therapy sessions.

Is it evidence-based?

Yes. Naturalistic, play-based approaches are well supported by research and blend behavioral science with child development. We support communication in all its forms, including speech, gestures, signs, AAC, and other individualized communication systems when appropriate.

How PBG delivers it

At PBG, we use your child's interests as the starting point for learning. Therapy takes place in the environments where your child lives, plays, and interacts with the people who matter most. We also work closely with parents so new skills can be practiced during mealtimes, play, community outings, and other parts of daily life.

Parent Coaching & Family Support

Your child's most important teacher is you.

We believe caregivers are experts on their own children. Our role is to work alongside you, combining what you know about your child with what we know about learning, communication, and development. Our goal is not to turn parents into therapists. Our goal is to help caregivers feel confident supporting communication, participation, independence, and learning during the moments that already matter most to family life.

We'll work with you to find practical strategies that fit your family's routines and priorities. We focus on the moments that already happen in your family's day, not on creating more things for you to do.

Why Family Involvement Matters

Children learn best when the important adults in their lives work together. When families are actively involved:

  • Children are more likely to use their skills at home, at school, and in the community.
  • Everyday routines become opportunities for learning and connection.
  • Parents and caregivers feel more confident supporting their child.
  • Children receive more consistent support from the people they spend the most time with.

Coaching Within Everyday Life

We help families build skills during routines they already experience every day, including:

Play & Connection

Using play to encourage communication, social engagement, shared enjoyment, and meaningful participation.

Mealtimes & Daily Living Skills

Building independence, flexibility, and participation during everyday routines.

Community Activities

Supporting success at the park, grocery store, family outings, and other meaningful experiences.

Transitions & Challenging Moments

Developing practical strategies for routines such as getting ready, leaving the house, or bedtime.

Working Together Across Settings

With your permission, we collaborate with teachers, school teams, and other providers to support consistency across environments. Our goal is to help everyone focus on the same skills and the same priorities for your child.

Supporting Skills That Last

A skill isn't very useful if it only happens during therapy. We want children to use what they're learning during real life with the people who matter most to them. Parent coaching helps make that possible by creating opportunities for learning throughout your child's day, not just during therapy sessions.

What Parent Coaching at PBG Looks Like

  • Practicing strategies during real routines such as playtime, meals, outings, and bedtime
  • Working together to solve challenges that matter to your family
  • Adjusting approaches based on your child's strengths and needs
  • Supporting the goals that are most important to your family
  • Celebrating progress and troubleshooting challenges together

You know your child better than anyone. Our role is to listen, share what we've learned, and work alongside you as you help your child grow and learn.

How Many Hours of Support Are Right for My Child?

The right amount of support depends on your child's strengths, needs, goals, and family priorities.

Many families worry they'll be given a number of therapy hours before anyone takes the time to understand their child. That's not how we work.

One of the first questions families ask is, "How many hours will my child need?" The answer is different for every child. At Partnership Behavior Group, we don't begin with a preset number of hours. We begin by getting to know your child, your family's priorities, and the outcomes that matter most to you.

There Is No "Correct" Number of Hours

Some children benefit from focused support targeting a small number of goals. Others benefit from a more comprehensive plan that addresses several developmental areas at once. The best recommendation is the one that helps your child make meaningful progress while fitting realistically into your family's daily life.

Why Early Support Matters

Young children learn through play, relationships, and everyday experiences. When support is introduced early, children often have more opportunities to develop communication, social engagement, independence, and participation in family and community life.

Our goal isn't simply to teach isolated skills. Our goal is to help children build skills that matter in real-world settings and support long-term success.

Our Commitment to Families

Our goal isn't to recommend as many hours as possible. Our goal is to recommend the right level of support at the right time, helping your child build meaningful skills and participate more fully in everyday life.

Ready to Learn More?

We won't recommend services until we understand your child, your family's priorities, and what meaningful success looks like for you.

What Is ABA?

Applied Behavior Analysis, in plain language.

Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) is a science devoted to understanding how people learn and how environments influence behavior. In practice, ABA helps children develop skills that support greater participation, communication, independence, and quality of life.

For many children with autism, ABA can help build important developmental skills such as communication, social interaction, play, daily living skills, and flexibility. Effective ABA begins with understanding each child's strengths, interests, and support needs, then creating individualized strategies that help them succeed in meaningful ways.

At its best, ABA is not about changing who a child is. It's about helping children access opportunities, connect with others, communicate their needs, and participate more fully in the activities and relationships that matter to them.

Modern ABA Looks Different

Some families have heard descriptions of ABA that involve repetitive drills, rigid teaching, or highly structured programs. While those approaches influenced earlier generations of practice, today's best ABA is often naturalistic, relationship-centered, and individualized.

At Partnership Behavior Group, learning happens through play, everyday routines, and meaningful interactions. We follow children's interests, build on their strengths, and create opportunities for learning within the activities they already enjoy. Therapy should feel engaging, supportive, and relevant to everyday life.

What can ABA help with?

Every child is unique, but ABA may support:

  • Communication – expressing wants, needs, ideas, and feelings
  • Social Connection – building relationships with family members, peers, and others
  • Play Skills – learning through exploration, imagination, and shared experiences
  • Daily Living Skills – increasing independence during everyday routines
  • Learning Readiness – developing skills that support participation at home, school, and in the community
  • Safety and Challenging Behavior – understanding the reasons behaviors occur and teaching effective alternatives

Who provides ABA?

ABA is directed by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA) — and at PBG, by a doctoral-level BCBA-D — who designs and oversees your child's plan. Day-to-day sessions are delivered by trained behavior technicians, with the clinician guiding progress and coaching your family throughout.

How PBG does ABA differently

ABA services are designed and supervised by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst (BCBA). At Partnership Behavior Group, services are overseen by a Board Certified Behavior Analyst-Doctoral (BCBA-D) and delivered by a team of trained professionals working closely with families.

Parent partnership is a core part of the process. We believe the most meaningful progress happens when children are supported not only during therapy sessions, but throughout their everyday lives by the people who know and love them best.

The PBG Approach

Partnership Behavior Group was founded on the belief that families deserve individualized, relationship-centered support. We provide services in homes and community settings where children naturally learn, play, and grow. Our focus is not on delivering a standardized program. It is on understanding your child, partnering with your family, and helping create meaningful outcomes that improve everyday life.

Why We Started Partnership Behavior Group

Families should never have to choose between the supports their child needs.

Partnership Behavior Group was founded on a simple belief: families deserve a true partner, not just a service provider.

That belief became especially clear while Dr. Dailey was serving as a Director of Special Education in Monterey County. A parent reached out after being told that receiving ABA services would require withdrawing her child from school in order to accommodate a provider's schedule.

The family had been placed in an impossible position. Services that should have been working together to support a child were instead competing with one another.

It reinforced something Dr. Dailey had observed throughout his career: children do best when the adults supporting them work together.

Partnership Behavior Group was created to provide a different experience for families. We believed there was a need for a practice that would work alongside families, schools, and other professionals rather than asking families to navigate those systems on their own.

Today, we continue to be guided by that same belief.
